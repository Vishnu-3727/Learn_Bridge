Photosynthesis: How Plants Make Their Own Food

Every living thing needs energy to stay alive. Animals get energy by eating other living things. Plants are different. A plant makes its own food inside its leaves, using nothing but sunlight, water and air. This process is called photosynthesis.

The word photosynthesis comes from two Greek words. "Photo" means light, and "synthesis" means putting together. A plant puts food together using light.

Where it happens

Photosynthesis happens mainly in the leaves. If you look at a leaf under a microscope, you can see tiny structures called chloroplasts inside its cells. Each chloroplast holds a green substance called chlorophyll. Chlorophyll is what makes leaves look green, and it is also what captures the energy in sunlight.

What the plant needs

A plant needs three things to carry out photosynthesis. It needs carbon dioxide, which it takes from the air through small openings in its leaves called stomata. It needs water, which its roots absorb from the soil and carry up the stem. And it needs light, usually from the sun.

What the plant produces

Using the captured light energy, the plant combines carbon dioxide and water to form glucose, which is a kind of sugar. Glucose stores the energy the plant will use later to grow, to make flowers, and to produce seeds. Oxygen is also produced, and the plant releases it into the air through the stomata.

This is why forests matter so much. Almost all the oxygen we breathe was released by plants and by algae in the ocean during photosynthesis.

Why it matters to everyone

Photosynthesis is the starting point of nearly every food chain on Earth. A goat eats grass, and a tiger eats the goat, but the energy in all three came originally from sunlight captured by a plant. When we eat rice or wheat, we are eating stored sunlight.

Plants also remove carbon dioxide from the air. Carbon dioxide traps heat, so by absorbing it, plants help keep the planet's temperature stable. Cutting down large numbers of trees reduces this natural cooling and makes climate change worse.

A common misunderstanding

Many people think plants only release oxygen and never use it. This is not true. Plants also carry out respiration, just as animals do, and respiration uses oxygen and releases carbon dioxide. The difference is that during daylight a healthy plant produces far more oxygen through photosynthesis than it consumes through respiration. At night, when there is no light, photosynthesis stops but respiration continues.
